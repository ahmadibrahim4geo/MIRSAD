# Output field dictionary clarification — 19 October 2026

The earlier two-column equation tables reused ambiguous explanations across
annual, seasonal and monthly fields. The replacement dictionary explains all
521 possible calculation fields in all 19 modules, in Arabic and English.

Each table row now includes the unchanged technical key, a readable name,
the exact period and named months, the calculation unit, and a field-specific
operation. The dictionary is documentation code in
`scripts/help_field_reference.py`; unknown field structures fail generation.
`formula_review.json` continues to check the unchanged scientific functions,
constants and actual emitted field sets.

The review distinguishes:

- Annual means, calendar-month means, season means and ranges between years
  for the same calendar month. An annual range compares monthly climatologies;
  it does not compare annual totals across years.
- Annual/seasonal precipitation means of complete-period totals. Concise map
  titles such as “Mean precipitation” remain unchanged.
- Annual and seasonal PET sums from monthly climatological calculations, even
  though their historical technical keys end in `Mean`. Monthly PET, actual
  per-year PET used for optional monthly ranges, and actual evapotranspiration
  are distinguished. No formula or field name changed.
- Maximum/minimum temperature means, warmest/coldest monthly means, circular
  wind direction, daily solar energy versus annual energy, Humidex in the
  winter HI field, signed water balance, and percentage-point ranges.
- Annual/monthly/seasonal trends and anomalies, rainfall percentages, and the
  five summaries of already calculated monthly trend/anomaly columns.

The symbols page now explains abbreviations and field-name components. The
outputs page separates reference-table rows from actual point/station records
and Daily variable/date rows, and explains identity, coordinates, source,
coverage, status, gap-fill and period-summary columns. This Desktop dictionary
does not assert identical Toolbox metadata layouts.

All channels consume the same generated content: native Desktop help, its
standalone help mode, offline/browser HTML and the two compiled CHM files.
Native Qt uses wrapping at word boundaries or within long technical names.
